# Pilot statistics: what 130 phone numbers can prove

Method: two-proportion z-test, 80% power, alpha 0.05 two-sided. MDE = minimum detectable lift in percentage points of appeal gift rate.

| Design | n (A / B) | MDE at 10% base | MDE at 15% base | MDE at 25% base |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Randomized 65 / 65 | 65 / 65 | 18.9 pts | 20.8 pts | 23.0 pts |
| 2. All 130 called vs 1,270 not called | 130 / 1,270 | 8.0 pts | 9.4 pts | 11.3 pts |
| 3. Phone append, randomized 315 / 315 | 315 / 315 | 7.7 pts | 8.8 pts | 10.2 pts |
| 4. Called group's own YoY change | 130 paired | descriptive only | descriptive only | descriptive only |

Example at 15% base, 65 / 65: need to go from 15% to ~36% to detect anything. A 65 / 65 test will almost surely show "no significant difference" even if the calls work.

**Design 2 is biased.** Donors with a phone on file are likely more engaged and give more at baseline. The ~9 pt MDE is false precision because any gap mixes the call effect with who these people are.

**Design 3.** Append gets ~9 pts at 15%. Still needs a big lift. Appended numbers are lower quality, so report both intent-to-treat and answered-only.

**Design 4 (call all 130, YoY).** Good for feasibility: connect rate, call length, opt-outs, complaints, sentiment. Rough descriptive signal on gift rate and average gift vs last year. Generates an effect-size guess for a bigger test. Cannot claim causation, no control group.

Bottom line: at 130, the pilot is a feasibility and experience exercise, not proof of lift. A real causal read needs 300+ per arm.
