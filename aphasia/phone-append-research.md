# Phone append: vendors, compliance, practical effect

Research Oct 22, 2026. Vendor pricing and match rates are mostly unpublished. Treat figures as partial.

## Vendors
- **The Databank:** residential phone append $15 per 1,000 records, $350 minimum, claims 25 to 50% match for DNC-exempt orgs. Canadian coverage not confirmed on the page. Ask before relying on it.
- **Data Axle Canada:** quote only, no published append pricing.
- **Melissa, Cleanlist, Equifax/TransUnion, iWave:** no published Canadian phone-append pricing found. Ask each for a sample match on ~500 records.
- Canada has no US-scale phone directories. Expect the low end of match rates, mostly landlines.

## Compliance for a live voice call to an appended number
- **CRTC Unsolicited Telecommunications Rules:** registered charities are exempt from the National DNCL. Still must register with the CRTC (free), keep an internal do-not-call list, call only in permitted hours (weekdays 9:00 to 21:30, weekends 10:00 to 18:00), identify caller and purpose at the start.
- **ADAD rules** (automatic dialing-announcing devices) may apply to an AI voice agent. Not verified. Counsel should check.
- **PIPEDA:** the donor never gave this number to the charity. New-purpose use strains reasonable expectations and consent. A privacy policy disclosing enrichment helps but does not fully solve it. Easy opt-out and a record of the number's source are needed.
- **CASL:** does not cover voice calls. Irrelevant.
- **Risk gap:** a pure thank-you call with no ask is lowest risk. A fundraising ask on an appended number is the clearest case for complaints and PIPEDA scrutiny. Keep the two separate.

## Practical effect
- No hard Canadian data comparing appended vs donor-provided numbers.
- Reasoning: appended numbers are often stale, shared or landline-only. Lower contact rate, more wrong-person calls. Donor-provided numbers carry implied permission. Appended ones can land as "how did you get this number?"
